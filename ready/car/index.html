Buying a car

Ready to buy a car? Know your position before the lot does.

At a dealership your credit decides your rate before you sit down. Here is what they look at, and what you can change first.

Take the free assessmentRead on

(01)The bands

How lenders sort you before you speak.

Auto lending runs on bands. Which one your file falls in decides the rate you are shown, and that happens before any negotiation about the car.

Deep subprime

The tiers lenders commonly use. Each lender sets its own cut offs and its own pricing, and these are not Smart Start’s categories.

(02)What changes the rate

Three levers, and you control two of them today.

01

Your band

The strongest single influence on what you are offered, and the slowest of the three to change.

02

Your deposit

More money down means less borrowed, which lowers both the risk and the monthly payment. It is immediate.

03

The term

A longer term makes the monthly figure look kinder and the total cost larger. The monthly payment is not the price.

(03)Fix first, then shop

Three things worth checking before you apply.

None of these are guaranteed to move anything. All three are worth an hour before you commit to years of payments.

01

An error on the file

Something reported wrongly is the one problem that can be put right rather than waited out. It is also the most common.

02

A card near its limit

Utilisation is the fastest moving factor there is. A balance reduced before the statement date is visible the next time the lender reports.

03

An old collection

Worth understanding before a lender raises it. Know what it is, who owns it now, and whether it is even accurate.

(04)Timing

The clock is not yours, and the rate is for years.

Disputes run on the credit bureaus’ statutory clock. Buying before your file moves locks in the rate you are offered today for the life of the loan. That is the whole trade off, stated plainly.

(06)Questions

What people ask at the lot.

There is no single figure. Auto lenders set their own cut offs and price the loan by band, so the same car costs two people different amounts. What is consistent is that a weaker file costs more, over the whole term.

Often yes, and usually expensively. Expect a higher rate, a larger deposit, a shorter list of lenders, or all three. Before you accept that, it is worth knowing whether one fixable item is holding the file where it is.

If you need the car this week, that decision is already made. If you have a little time, finding out what is on your file first is cheap and can change what you are offered. Nobody can promise you that it will, and anybody who does is not allowed to.
