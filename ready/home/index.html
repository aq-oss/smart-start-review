Buying a home

Ready to buy a home? Here is how to know.

Readiness is not one number. It is your credit, your debt, your savings and your paperwork, read together. This page walks through each, honestly.

Take the free assessmentRead on

(01)What readiness means

Four questions a lender is really asking.

Every part of a mortgage decision comes back to one of these. Knowing which one is your weak point is most of the work.

  1. 01

    Can you qualify?

    Your credit file decides whether a lender will lend at all, and on what terms.

  2. 02

    Can you carry it?

    Your income against your monthly obligations decides how much a lender thinks you can hold.

  3. 03

    Can you close?

    The deposit is not the whole bill. Closing costs and reserves are part of being ready.

  4. 04

    Can you prove it?

    Identity, income and history have to be evidenced, not asserted.

(02)Your credit

What lenders look at.

Lenders describe credit in bands rather than in single numbers. Where you sit affects whether you are offered a loan at all, and what it costs you if you are.

Some of what sits in a band can be fixed: an error, an old collection that should have aged off, a card running close to its limit. Some of it only time fixes, such as the length of your history.

Worth knowing

A lender may use a different score from the one an app shows you. Two scores of the same file can disagree, because they are different models built for different questions.

(03)Your debt

The ratio behind the decision.

Debt to income compares what you owe every month against what you earn every month. It is the lender’s test of whether you can carry a payment, and it is often the thing that stops an application that looked fine on credit alone.

Lenders publish their own limits and programs differ, so no single percentage is the rule. What matters is knowing your own figure before somebody else works it out for you.

(04)Savings and paperwork

What you will be asked to show.

Evidence

Proof of income, proof of identity, and statements covering the recent past. Money that arrives without a trail tends to create questions rather than answer them.

Money beyond the deposit

Closing costs, moving costs, and reserves left over afterwards. Being ready means the purchase does not empty you out on the day it completes.

(05)Where people get stuck

Three blockers, and what to do about each.

Almost everyone who stalls stalls on one of these. Usually only one.

01

An error, or an old collection

Something on the file that should not be there, or should have aged off. What to do: get the report, read it line by line, and dispute what is wrong.

02

A date that does not fit the surplus

The goal is fine. The timing is not, on the money that is actually spare each month. What to do: move the date, or change the surplus.

03

Applying before the file is ready

An application made too early can cost you for years. What to do: fix the one thing doing the most damage first, then apply.
