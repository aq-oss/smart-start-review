What is hurting your credit score.

Five factors decide most of it. Here is what each one is, what damages it, what helps, and which of them you can actually move.

In short

  • Payment history and how much you owe carry most of the weight.
  • Utilisation is the fastest thing to change. History is the slowest.
  • Usually one item is doing most of the damage, not all five at once.

Payment history and amounts owed together are described by FICO as about two thirds of the score.

(02)Payment history

Payment history

Whether you have paid your accounts on time, and what happened when you did not.

What hurts it

A missed payment reported to the bureau, an account in collection, a charge off, a default.

What helps

Paying on time, every time, for a long time. There is no shortcut in this one.

How quickly it moves

A late payment loses weight gradually as it ages, but it stays on the file for years.

(03)Amounts owed

Amounts owed

How much of your available credit you are using, on each account and across all of them.

What hurts it

Running a card close to its limit, even when you clear it in full each month.

What helps

Lowering the balance, or raising the limit, before the statement date rather than after it.

How quickly it moves

This is the fastest moving factor of the five. It can change within a reporting cycle.

(04)Length of history

Length of history

How long your accounts have been open, and the average age across them.

What hurts it

Closing your oldest card. Opening several new accounts at once, which drags the average down.

What helps

Time, and leaving old accounts open where there is no cost to keeping them.

How quickly it moves

Only time changes this. Nothing you can buy will accelerate it.

(05)New credit

New credit

How much new borrowing you have applied for recently.

What hurts it

A burst of applications in a short window, each leaving a hard inquiry.

What helps

Applying deliberately rather than hopefully, and leaving gaps between applications.

How quickly it moves

Inquiries lose weight within months and drop off the file within two years.

(06)Credit mix

Credit mix

Whether you have handled different kinds of credit, such as cards and instalment loans.

What hurts it

Very little on its own. It is the smallest of the five.

What helps

Nothing worth taking on debt for. Mix improves as a by product of ordinary borrowing.

How quickly it moves

Slow, and rarely the thing worth fixing first.

(07)The common puzzle

Why is my score low when I pay on time?

Five explanations cover almost every case. Work down the list.

01

High utilisation

You pay in full, but the balance reported on the statement date is close to the limit. The bureau sees the balance, not your intention.

02

A thin file

Too little history to read. Scoring models are not rewarding you for being careful, because there is not enough to look at.

03

An error

An account that is not yours, a payment marked late that was not, or a balance that was settled and never updated.

04

A collection you did not know about

An old bill, often medical or a final utility bill, sold on and reported by somebody you have never heard of.

05

A burst of inquiries

Several applications in a short window, each one leaving a mark, read together as pressure.

(08)The largest obstacle

Usually it is one thing.

People arrive expecting a list of everything wrong with them. In practice one item is normally doing most of the damage, and the rest is noise around it. Fixing the noise first feels productive and changes very little.

Naming that one item is what the free assessment is for. It does not pull your report and it takes about three minutes.
